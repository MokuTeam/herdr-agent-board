Threads mockups for #379

A card shows the threads its session works on

The board as it is today, with two additions: the Threads pill in the toolbar, counting what needs you, and a row of thread chips on a card. A chip is the thread's status dot and its key, and it opens the thread. A card whose session has no thread does not change.

The threads panel: the list on the left, one thread on the right

Built like the Team panel and from its parts. The list is grouped by the five statuses, which are the card's five columns in the same colours. Pick any row: the three sub-threads of atlas-12, the thread shared with ben (atlas-16, with an agent's file held for you), the proposed close (atlas-10) and a thread with no feed (atlas-17) each show a different state. Chips and links say where they lead.

On the phone the list and the thread are two screens

One column. A thread is pushed over the list from the right and leaves the same way; the back arrow returns to the list where you left it. The panel is the board's full-height sheet, inset 12 px, as every panel is on the phone.

MokuPilot shows the board's threads where its task board was

Drawn in MokuPilot's own palette, since this view is built in its Qt window and not on the board's page. The same threads as on the board, read through the board. The sections MokuPilot has today stay (Now, Handed off, Next, Done) and two are new (Needs you, Waiting). An owner chip is a button now: it opens that session on the board. In the chat, the two blue lines are everything the day orchestrator received about the work; a thread key anywhere in the chat opens the thread. Click a row.

An item opens into its thread

The list slides out and the thread comes in, in the same panel: the head with its status, who holds it, the parent and the link to the board; then the feed, with a question for you as a block you answer in place. "Threads" at the top goes back.

The inspector at its narrowest

MokuPilot is a desktop app and has no phone build. This is its inspector alone at 360 px, the width a phone would give it: rows keep one line of title, and a thread uses the full width.
